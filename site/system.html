System card: Kapé Lumen Assistant

1. What it does

Lumen is a chat assistant on the Kapé Lumen café website. You type a question about the café, such as prices, opening hours, the loyalty card or house rules, and it answers in a sentence or two using only the café's own documents. If the documents do not say, it tells you to ask the staff instead of guessing.

2. Who uses it, and who must not

  • Intended users: customers and would-be customers of the (fictional) café, looking for everyday information before or during a visit.
  • Explicitly NOT for: allergy, dietary or medical advice; ordering, reservations or payment; anything involving personal data; any decision about an identifiable person. Children should not use it without an adult, as with any public chat service.

5. Data

  • Where it came from: written for this project; all invented (content/*.md in the repository, synced to a versioned S3 bucket).
  • Size and format: 5 Markdown files, about 7.5 KB.
  • Anything personal in it: no; all invented. Addresses and emails are marked "(fictional)".
  • Sent to a third-party AI service: the café documents and the customer's question go to Google Gemini. Users are told in the terms pop-up not to type personal data, and the documents contain none.

6. How good it is

  • The one number: 10 of 10 test questions correct: on 2026-11-06 (local) and on AWS on 2026-11-07 05:46 UTC, and every night since by the automatic quality check.
  • How measured: eval/run-eval.mjs sends each question through the real gateway and model, and checks the answer for expected phrases (for example "170" for the latte price) and forbidden ones (for example "safe for you" on the allergy question).
  • What the 10 cover: opening hours (2), menu price, loyalty stamps, pets, an allergy question that must be refused, the Wi-Fi password (which it must not know), reservations, student discount, and an out-of-scope request (ordering pizza from next door) that it must decline.
  • An example it gets wrong: on 2026-11-06 the same allergy question that normally gets a safe refusal returned an error instead, because the free model quota (20 requests a day on gemini-3.5-flash) was used up; see test 3 on Results. Ten questions is also a small sample: a phrase check can pass an answer that is technically correct but badly worded.

7. How it is served

  • Where it runs: one EC2 t3.small (2 vCPU, 2 GB RAM), ap-southeast-1 (Singapore), four Docker containers: Caddy (HTTPS), LibreChat (the chat app), MongoDB (accounts and conversations) and brew-gateway (my small service between LibreChat and the model).
  • What calls it: customers' browsers at https://47-131-78-149.sslip.io; LibreChat calls brew-gateway's OpenAI-style /v1/chat/completions.
  • The model: Google Gemini gemini-3.5-flash-lite, free tier.
  • If the model is unavailable: brew-gateway returns a clear message ("The assistant is busy… try again in a minute", or "The AI model did not respond"), logs the failure, and the kape-answers-failing alarm emails me when 3 or more fail within 5 minutes.

8. Limitations and risks

  • What it cannot do: know anything not in the five documents (today's specials, stock, queue length); take orders or bookings; remember who you are between visits beyond your saved chat history.
  • Free-tier limits: the model has per-minute and per-day request limits. Busy periods will hit them, and customers will see "busy" messages. That is acceptable for a demo, not for a real café.
  • What could go wrong with the data: if a document is out of date (a price changes), Lumen will confidently quote the old price. The documents are versioned in S3 and in git, but someone has to update them.
  • Wrong answers: language models can still make things up or misread a document, especially for questions the documents only half answer.
  • How it could be misused: people may try to make it say things outside its role (prompt injection), or paste personal data into it. It has no tools or access to anything except the documents, so the harm is limited to a bad answer.
  • What a user must always check themselves: anything about allergies or ingredients (ask the staff in person), and prices and holiday hours before relying on them.

Well-Architected review

I reviewed the system against the six pillars of the AWS Well-Architected Framework twice: the first pass on 2026-11-07, as soon as the deployment worked (HTTPS, secrets and IAM in place, but no monitoring or backups yet), and the second pass on the same day after the monitoring, backups, automations and the incident fix. Verdicts: good, adequate or gap.

The trade-off I chose

Optimised for: Security and Operational Excellence. A café assistant holds customer accounts and runs on a free model key, so the costly failures are a leaked key, an exposed database or a problem nobody notices. Those are cheap to prevent on one server, so I made them strong first.

Accepted as weaker: Reliability (availability) and Performance under load. Two servers behind a load balancer, or a managed database in two Availability Zones, would multiply the cost of a system that serves a handful of users, and the course limits the footprint. If the server dies, the data is safe and can be restored, but the site is down until it is rebuilt or AWS recovers it. A paid model tier would remove the request limits, but there is no budget for it.

The one gap I would fix first

Everything (app, database, uploads and the newest local backups) lives on one server's disk in one Availability Zone. The nightly backup limits data loss to 24 hours, but rebuilding takes about 30 minutes of manual work. I would first add daily EBS snapshots with Amazon Data Lifecycle Manager, kept 7 days: it costs cents a month and needs no code, it cuts the rebuild to launching from a snapshot in minutes, and it also protects the server's configuration, not just the database. It is the largest improvement in recovery per peso spent.
